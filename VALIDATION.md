# credit-risk-lab: model validation pack

Structured after OSFI Guideline E-23 (model risk management, final September 2025, effective May 1, 2027) and the US Federal Reserve's SR 11-7 guidance on model risk management. Generated by `src/validation_pack.py`: every computed figure comes from the pipeline run or from tests computed in that script against `results/results.json`. Nothing is typed in.

## 2. Conceptual soundness

- Target and features are behaviourally coherent: payment status (PAY_0..PAY_6), bill and payment amounts, and engineered behavioural features (utilization, payment ratio, delinquency mean/max, bill trend). No post-outcome fields are used as inputs; the target is next month's default and every feature is observed at or before the current statement.
- Logistic regression is retained as the interpretable baseline a validation function expects: if the complex model cannot beat a transparent one out of sample, the complex one does not ship. Gradient boosting is the challenger that earned champion status on held-out discrimination.
- Known design weakness, stated openly: six months of payment history enter as levels, and the outcome is one month ahead on the same accounts. This is behavioural scoring on an existing book, not application scoring of new applicants, and the metrics should be read that way.

## 6. Benchmarking

Published results on this exact dataset (30,000 accounts, 22.12% default), from RESEARCH.md: the strongest leakage-safe published gradient boosting reaches AUC 0.7878 to 0.7880 (Research Square preprint, September 2026, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10794000/v1); an optimized CatBoost reports AUC 0.7814, KS 0.4324, Brier 0.1350. This lab's champion: AUC 0.7810, KS 0.4196, Brier 0.1355, within 0.007 AUC of the best published number on the same data, from a plain pipeline with no leakage and no tuning search. Production application scorecards typically report AUC 0.70 to 0.80; behavioural books score higher because the target and features are richer.

## 7. Ongoing monitoring plan

- On any new data vintage: recompute score PSI and per-feature CSI against the development sample; log values beside the customary 0.10 / 0.25 reference zones, treated as signals to investigate, not automatic pass/fail.
- Quarterly outcomes analysis on booked accounts: AUC, KS, Brier, and the calibration table by score decile; a Hosmer-Lemeshow rejection triggers a recalibration review, not a silent refit.
- Annual full revalidation in this document's structure, plus revalidation on any material change (features, target window, portfolio mix).
- Fairness monitoring with the study machinery (`src/study.py`): approval rates and AUC by group at the production cut-off, with group sizes always printed.

## 8. Limitations, stated honestly

- **No true out-of-time split exists in this dataset.** The data is a single 2005 cross-section with a one-month outcome; the train/test split is random, not temporal. Published work shows in-sample AUC around 0.82 decaying to about 0.70 out of time, so these metrics overstate what a production deployment should expect.
- **PSI thresholds are rules of thumb.** The 0.10 / 0.25 zones have no Type I/II error basis (Yurdakul and Naranjo, 2020, Journal of Risk Model Validation). The lab reports PSI as a signal with its value attached, never as a law.
- **LGD is assumed, not modelled.** ECL uses a flat 45% LGD and a 3-year lifetime approximation for Stage 2. Both are labelled assumptions; the Basel QRRE foundation LGD floor of 50% is shown beside them in `results/scenarios.json`.
- The Hosmer-Lemeshow test is sensitive to grouping and sample size; it is one calibration lens among several, reported with its statistic rather than as a verdict.
- This is a public-data lab. It is not a production system, not validated by any institution, and not employment experience in credit risk.
